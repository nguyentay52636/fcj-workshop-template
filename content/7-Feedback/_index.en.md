> After participating in the First Cloud AI Journey program, I had the opportunity to learn quite a few new things — not only about AWS and AI, but also about how to work effectively, self-study, and proactively solve problems. Below are some reflections based on my real experience throughout the program.

### Overall Evaluation

**1. Working Environment**

I found the environment in the program to be quite friendly and open. During the internship, there were times when I encountered problems I didn't know how to handle, especially when working with AWS and services I had little prior experience with. Whenever I faced difficulties, I could discuss with teammates or reach out to my mentor.

What I appreciated most was that no one made me feel pressured when I didn't know something. This made me more comfortable asking questions and exploring on my own.

In addition, the regular check-ins and periodic discussions helped me understand where I stood in the internship and what I needed to complete next.

**2. Support from Mentor / Team Admin**

Throughout the internship, I received a great deal of support from both the mentor and team admin. Especially in the early stages, when I was still unfamiliar with the AWS ecosystem, being guided on the right approach and reference materials helped me learn independently rather than waiting for direct answers.

Some problems took me quite a while to figure out, requiring multiple attempts before I could resolve them. In hindsight, this process was also what helped me develop a deeper understanding of the material.

I'm also grateful to the team admin for their support with account-related issues, access permissions, and other matters that came up throughout the program.

**3. Relevance of Work to Academic Background**

The work in the program was fairly well-aligned with my academic direction, while also giving me the opportunity to explore knowledge areas I hadn't had much chance to practice before.

Before joining, I mostly had foundational knowledge and didn't fully understand how different AWS components could work together in a real project. During the internship, I was introduced to concepts like RAG, Lambda, Bedrock, Infrastructure as Code, and system quality evaluation.

What I found most valuable was that I wasn't just learning individual technologies — I started to understand how they fit together to form a complete system.

**4. Team Culture and Spirit**

I sensed a strong spirit of mutual support among program members. Even though everyone had different levels of progress and understanding, people were still willing to exchange ideas and help each other when issues arose.

As an intern, being in an environment where I felt comfortable asking questions was something I truly valued. Some problems initially seemed quite difficult, but after discussing them with others, I gained new directions to keep exploring.

**5. Internship Policies and Experience**

Overall, I felt the program had a fairly clear roadmap and provided interns with plenty of learning materials for self-study. Having structured learning content and hands-on requirements at each phase gave me more direction rather than having to figure everything out on my own from scratch.

However, during the program I also realized that having a clear roadmap doesn't mean I could simply follow steps passively. I still needed to actively read requirements carefully, manage my own progress, and review what I had completed.

This is also one of the areas I recognized I need to improve.

---

### Additional Questions

* **What did you find most satisfying during your internship?**

What I found most satisfying was the opportunity to participate in a real project rather than just studying theory. While building the RAG Knowledge Assistant, I had the chance to see an initial idea develop into a system with many different components.

I particularly enjoyed the feeling of independently researching a problem, experimenting, encountering errors, and then finding solutions. There were moments that were quite challenging and time-consuming, but after resolving them, the knowledge I gained felt much more meaningful.

* **What do you think the program could improve for future interns?**

Based on my personal experience, I think the program could provide clearer guidance on the completion criteria for each type of report or deliverable from the very beginning.

During the internship, I sometimes focused heavily on the technical and project work while not paying enough attention to the requirements around documenting the learning process, sharing, and summarizing results.

If these requirements were explained more clearly with a checklist or specific examples from the start, I think interns would find it easier to track their progress and avoid missing anything.

I also recognize that this is my own responsibility for reading and checking requirements carefully — a lesson I believe I'll need to carry forward into future roles.

* **If recommending the program to a friend, would you suggest they join? Why?**

Yes. I think this is a program well-suited for those who want to explore AWS, Cloud, and AI more deeply through hands-on project work.

However, I also think participants need a strong spirit of initiative and self-directed learning. Some problems won't have ready-made answers, so the ability to find documentation independently, experiment, and persistently work through challenges is very important.

---

### Suggestions & Expectations

If given the opportunity to provide feedback for future programs, I hope the guidance on output requirements and reports can be presented more specifically — especially for deliverables that interns need to complete throughout the entire program.

Additionally, I think having a few examples of complete reports or a phase-by-phase checklist would help interns self-assess more easily — especially a simple **Done / Partial / Deferred** tracker so incomplete loops (like a skeleton evaluation job) are declared honestly instead of sounding finished.

On my part, after the program I realized that technical knowledge is only one piece. The ability to manage work, read requirements carefully, track progress, and verify results before completing a task is equally important.

I'd like to thank my mentor, the team admin, and all the members of the program who supported me throughout this time. It was an experience from which I learned a great deal — both technically and in terms of how to work and take responsibility for my own output.

I hope the knowledge and experience gained from this program will serve as a foundation for my continued growth going forward.
